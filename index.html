About

About Me

Hello! This is Md. Nazmus Sakib, a B.Sc. graduate in Agricultural Science from Sher-e-Bangla Agricultural University with a strong passion for sustainable agriculture and crop production.

I am currently pursuing MSc in Agronomy at the same university. My academic journey has been marked by excellence, reflected in receiving the Dean’s Award because of obtaining CGPA 3.88/4 in my bachelor degree.

Serving as class representative throughout my university years allowed me to develop strong leadership, organizational, and communication skills.

Research Vision

I am particularly interested in exploring molecular biology and crop stress physiology to enhance crop yield, improve resilience, and develop innovative solutions for sustainable agriculture.

My goal is to translate scientific knowledge into practical solutions that support national development, improve food security, and promote environmentally responsible farming practices.

I am eager to connect with fellow researchers, professionals, and organizations in agriculture and life sciences.

Research Interests

Primary Research Interests

Agronomy, Crop Science, Plant Stress Physiology


I am aiming at developing new holistic approaches to enhance crop production under stressful environmental conditions.


  • Salinity stress
  • Extreme temperatures
  • Soil acidity
  • Drought
  • Waterlogging
  • Nutritional disorders
  • Biotic stresses

The ultimate goal of my research is to develop novel strategies and assist breeders in developing germplasm to enhance crop adaptability and resilience under stressful environmental conditions and improve crop yield to achieve agricultural sustainability.

Problems Being Addressed

Investigating complex mechanisms of concurrent soil salinity and waterlogging stress in wheat, focusing on physiological bottlenecks, stomatal conductance, ion homeostasis, oxidative stress response under controlled environments.

Progress & Achieved Results

Successfully integrated and analyzed experimental data points to derive key physiological metrics. Currently evaluating stress-tolerant wheat lines to map root-shoot biomass traits and physiological markers for climate-smart breeding.
